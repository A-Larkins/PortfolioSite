I track a mess of yearly goals — disc golf stats, finance targets, entertainment challenges, general life stuff — and none of the note-taking apps I tried fit the shape of "check things off or fill in a number as the year goes." So I built the tracker the shape I actually wanted.

Swift and SwiftUI, one app for iOS and macOS, with everything you touch synced through a single local state file.

What it does:

• Three item types cover almost anything: a plain checkbox for one-off goals, a fill-in blank for a number you settle at year end, and an anniversary line that auto-advances every year on its own.<br>
• Every year gets its own tab — the current one editable, every past year archived and still browsable — with full add/rename/delete on milestones, groups, and categories, current or past.<br>
• Milestones carry a real-world date separate from when you checked the box, so a wedding backdates to when it actually happened rather than to whenever you got around to recording it.<br>
• "Close Year" archives the year and starts the next one — unfinished items and anniversaries roll forward automatically, achieved ones stay put in history.<br>
• A report tab renders a PDF summary of everything accomplished, grouped by category, cached until you ask it to regenerate.

It turned a scattered set of yearly resolutions into one place that actually remembers what I did.

Federal student loan interest accrues every second whether you're watching or not. I wanted a dashboard that made that fact impossible to ignore — and that told me, every month, whether I was actually winning.

Python and Tkinter, single-file, packaged as a macOS app. Sibling project to an earlier budget app of mine, same bones: JSON storage, a one-second tick, no third-party dependencies.

What it does:

• Instead of simulating amortization from origination, it works off snapshots — you enter the real current principal from your servicer, dated, and it accrues simple daily interest forward from there until the next snapshot resyncs it.<br>
• The centerpiece is "the race": interest accrued this month versus what you've paid, split by a break-even line, so you know in real time whether you're ahead of the interest or behind it.<br>
• Loans are ranked into a kill order — highest rate first, smallest balance breaking ties — so it's obvious which one to attack. Autopay hits the account rather than a loan, and rather than guess at a proportional split, the app cascades the payment down that same kill order, which is what the real transaction history turned out to show.<br>
• A live rate ladder plots every loan against CPI-U pulled from the BLS API, so whether a given loan is actually costing you in real terms is a glance rather than a calculation.<br>
• The burn is stated per day, per month, and per year — the yearly figure is the one that actually argues for a bigger payment.<br>
• Every payment gets an autopsy: how much went to interest versus principal, and how many days closer to freedom it moved you.<br>
• No real balance ever lives in the repo — the app writes to a path outside the project, and the schema example is entirely fake numbers.

It's a motivator, not an amortization schedule — the honest kind of estimate that stays close to truth because it resyncs every month instead of pretending to model years of history it never saw.

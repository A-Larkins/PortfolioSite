I'm working through <em>1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die</em> and tracking it in Google Sheets — the master list with my ratings, a starred "must hear" list, and my own replacement picks. Keeping those three in sync by hand meant copying rows between tabs and renumbering everything by hand. So I built an app to do it.

C# on .NET 10 with Avalonia, running as both a Mac desktop app and an iPhone app from one shared codebase. The two heads are thin — an entry point plus the platform's Avalonia backend — so all the real logic lives in one library.

What it does:

• Sync is a reconcile pass, not an overwrite — it works out what's actually different, adds starred albums missing from the must-hear list, drops rows that shouldn't be there, and places new entries by year before renumbering. A read-only check tells me what a sync would change before anything gets written.<br>
• Ratings (⭐ / 👍 / 👎 / ❌) as I listen, changeable after the fact from either device — by walking back through what I've already rated, or jumping straight to an album from the browse list.<br>
• Builds Apple Music playlists directly: the iPhone uses Apple's public MediaPlayer framework, and the Mac drives Music.app over AppleScript, both behind one interface.<br>
• A shortlist of replacement picks, validated against both lists so nothing lands twice, with Discogs lookups filling in missing years.<br>
• Searchable viewer and PDF export via QuestPDF.

The interesting constraint was sync on a phone. It talks to the Google Sheets REST API directly with a service-account JWT rather than the Google client libraries, which is what lets the phone sync with no OAuth browser sign-in at all. Apple Music was the other one: MediaPlayer is add-only — there's no public API to remove a track from a playlist — so removals become a checklist I work through by hand rather than something the app can pretend it did.

Built with AI assistance, which is most of why it exists — the idea had been sitting on my list for a long time.
